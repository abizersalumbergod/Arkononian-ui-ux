# Copywriting

Writing and editing the words of a site: how to diagnose what you were handed, how to draft page copy that is clear, specific and supportable, how headlines, sections and calls to action are built, and how to edit existing copy in seven focused sweeps. Facts and claims are never invented; a claim that cannot be supported is declined with the evidence that would unlock it.

**Load when:** writing, rewriting or editing visitor-facing words (home, landing, pricing, feature, about and product pages, headlines, calls to action, value propositions, taglines, hero copy, case studies), refreshing outdated content, or the user says copy is weak, vague or sounds like AI. **Owns:** the three input situations, the drafting protocol, the supportable-claim rule, headline and section formulas, page templates, call-to-action rules, message-market-fit tests, the seven sweeps, the expert panel, quick-pass checks, and content refresh.
**Related:** [prose-humanizing](prose-humanizing.md), [positioning-and-research](positioning-and-research.md), [conversion-optimization](conversion-optimization.md), [seo-and-discoverability](seo-and-discoverability.md), [landing-page-engineering](landing-page-engineering.md), [interaction-design](interaction-design.md), [product-discovery](product-discovery.md), [domain-model](domain-model.md), [growth-channels](growth-channels.md)

## Decision rules

1. **Gather before drafting.** Read the project's positioning notes and glossary first ([positioning-and-research](positioning-and-research.md), [domain-model](domain-model.md)); ask only for what is missing: the page's one primary action, the audience and the words they use for their problem and objections, the offer and what differs from alternatives, proof points, where traffic comes from and what visitors already know.
2. **Diagnose the situation and say it in one line, with evidence.** A: a blank or generic brief (build the argument from the brief and product facts; latitude on angle, never on facts). B: a real voice is present (restore it: keep their argument, vocabulary and emphasis; fix the buried lead, vague lines, tells and structure). C: a point edit (change only what was asked plus the minimum around it; return only the changed lines with a one-line reason each).
3. **Read taste out of the input (B and C).** Vocabulary is a voice choice ("stuff" and "ship" stay); where they spent words is a priority choice; what they left out is a scope choice; their angle is their positioning. A writing sample beats the default voice: match its sentence length, punctuation, openings and a few words you would not choose.
4. **Decompose and lock.** Do not write a page in one pass. Break it into named units (headline, subhead, call to action, each section heading and body, proof lines); for each, draft two or three candidates, keep the best, fix its worst flaw, lock it; then read the whole page once for flow. In situation B, lines that already work are locked from the start.
5. **The three-question gate for every line you write or keep:** can the reader picture it; can it be proven true or false; could a competitor write this exact sentence (the swap test)? Pass all three and keep it; pass two and sharpen it; pass one or none and rewrite from the facts or flag it with `[NEED: ...]`.
6. **Ration the punch.** Most lines are plain, specific and declarative. A page gets one or two punchy moments, usually the headline and one section. When every line tries to land, none does.
7. **The leave-it-alone test.** For each change, ask whether it is measurably better or only different; revert what you cannot give a reason for.
8. **Clarity over cleverness; benefits over features; specificity over vagueness; the customer's language over the company's; one idea per section.** If a reader must pause to decode a line, it has failed.
9. **Claims must be supportable, and a declined claim names its unlock.** Write only what the source material supports. When a requested claim is not supported, do not write it and do not silently drop it: name the claim, say why it falls short, offer the closest supportable alternative, and state the evidence that would unlock the original (a review export, a written guarantee policy, a certification, a citable study). Never invent statistics, customers, testimonials, logos, counts or urgency ([SKILL.md §2](../SKILL.md)).
10. **Facts supplied by the owner stay exact:** prices, numbers, names, quotations, legal and consent text.
11. **Drafts carry no machine tells.** Never write contrast reveals ("it's not X, it's Y"), negation lists ("no setup, no templates, no waiting"), trailing pile-ons after a finished claim, self-answered questions or colon reveals ("The result? 3x faster."), stock openers ("In today's fast-paced world", "Say goodbye to", "Unlock the power of"), or em dashes in headlines, subheads, buttons, ads and subject lines. Cap one fragment and one list of three per section. Treat seamless, robust, powerful, streamline and empower as placeholders for a fact. Then run the full pass in [prose-humanizing](prose-humanizing.md).
12. **Edit in focused sweeps,** one dimension per pass, looping back after each to confirm earlier sweeps still hold. A point edit skips the sweeps.
13. **Every edit has a reason, and the author's voice survives.** Do not change the core message; enhance it.
14. **Mark gaps instead of inventing:** `[NEED: differentiator]`, `[NEED: proof for this number]`, `[NEED: legal text from owner]`. Fix tells from the facts; swapping synonyms makes new tells.
15. **Deliver in the site's structure:** page copy by section, annotations for key choices, two or three alternatives for headlines and calls to action, and search title and meta description where relevant ([seo-and-discoverability](seo-and-discoverability.md)). UI microcopy follows [interaction-design](interaction-design.md).

## Specifications

### Page structure

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Headline | The single most important message; specific beats generic; it should pass the "Now you can" test below |
| Subheadline | One or two sentences that add specificity |
| Primary call to action | `[verb] + [what they get] + [qualifier if needed]`: "Start my free trial", "Get the complete checklist", "See pricing for my team". Avoid Submit, Sign up, Learn more, Click here, Get started on their own |
| Hero as a transformation | Current discomfort, then a better vision, then the path to action (below) |

Core sections: social proof (credibility), problem (show you understand), solution and benefits (three to five, not ten), how it works (three or four steps that reduce perceived complexity), objection handling (FAQ, comparison, guarantee), final call to action (recap the value, repeat the action, reverse the risk). Supporting sections to add where they earn their place: testimonials with names, roles and specific results; case studies (problem, solution, results); use cases and "built for" blocks; FAQ (the five to ten real objections); comparison with alternatives or the status quo; integrations; founder story; demo or tour; pricing preview; guarantee; stats. Choose by the visitor's open questions, not by a template.

### Headline formulas (starting points; fill every slot with specifics)

| Family | Formulas |
|---|---|
| Outcome | `{outcome} without {pain}`; `{outcome} by {mechanism}`; `Turn {input} into {outcome}`; `{outcome} in {timeframe}` |
| Problem | `Never {unpleasant event} again`; a question naming the pain; `Stop {pain}. Start {pleasure}.` |
| Audience | `{product type} for {audience}`; `You do not have to {skill} to {outcome}` |
| Differentiation | `The {category} that {differentiator}`; `The {opposite of the usual process} way to {outcome}` |
| Proof | `{number} {people} use {product} to {outcome}` (only with a real number) |

A formula headline is fine once; do not follow it with a negation list.

### Page templates

| Page | Order |
|---|---|
| Persuasive landing page | Hero with value proposition, proof bar, problem, how it works, two or three benefits, testimonial, use cases, comparison, case snippet, FAQ, final call to action with guarantee |
| Compact ad landing page | Hero, proof bar, three benefits, testimonial, how it works, final call to action with guarantee |
| Enterprise page | Outcome headline, recognisable logos, business problem, solution, use cases by role, security and compliance, integrations, case study with metrics, value, contact or demo |
| Launch page | Announcement hero, demo video, three to five highlights, before and after, early testimonials, launch offer, call to action (urgency only if real) |

A page that is only a hero followed by feature blocks is a list, not an argument.

### Message-market-fit tests

| Test | How |
|---|---|
| "Now you can" | Prefix the line with "Now you can...". If the result is compelling and true it works; if it is vague, obvious or a stretch, rewrite. "Streamline your workflow" fails; "see which companies visit your site" passes |
| Human action model | The hero names the felt discomfort in the reader's words, then the better state, then why this step closes the gap. Miss a beat and the reader stalls |
| Perception gap | The same benefit reads as a strength to one segment and a red flag to another ("move fast" to a start-up, "unproven" to a risk-averse buyer). Segment the traffic and write each its own truth rather than averaging them |

### Page-specific guidance

Home: serve several audiences without going generic, lead with the broadest value, give clear paths by intent. Landing: one message, one call to action, headline matched to the traffic source, a complete argument on the page. Pricing: help the visitor choose, answer "which is right for me?", make the recommended plan obvious. Feature: feature, then benefit, then outcome; use cases; a clear path to try or buy. About: why you exist, mission tied to customer benefit, still a call to action.

### Voice and tone

Decide formality (casual, professional but friendly, formal), personality (playful or serious, bold or understated, technical or accessible) and keep it consistent while varying intensity: headlines bolder, body clearer, calls to action action-oriented.

### The seven sweeps (edit one dimension at a time)

| # | Sweep | Question | Typical failure | After it, re-check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clarity | Can the reader understand it? | Overloaded sentences, jargon, unclear pronouns, buried points | One idea per section; speaks to the reader |
| 2 | Voice and tone | Does it sound consistent? | Casual that turns corporate; "we" mixed with "the company" | Clarity |
| 3 | So what | Does every claim answer "why should I care?" | Features without benefits; add the "which means..." bridge | Voice, clarity |
| 4 | Prove it | Is every claim supported? | "Trusted by thousands" (which?), "industry-leading" (by whom?) | So what, voice, clarity |
| 5 | Specificity | Is it concrete? | "Save time" instead of "save four hours a week"; round, made-up-looking numbers | Prove it, and the earlier sweeps |
| 6 | Heightened emotion | Does the reader feel something? | Flat, informational language; pain mentioned but not felt | Specificity and the earlier sweeps |
| 7 | Zero risk | Is every barrier to action removed? | Friction near the button, unanswered objections, vague "Contact us" | All sweeps once more |

Emotion serves the message and stays honest; it is never manipulation. Risk reducers (guarantees, free trials, "no card required", "cancel anytime", proof beside the button, clear next steps) are stated only if true.

### Expert panel (a quality gate for launch, pricing and high-traffic pages)

Assemble three to five viewpoints relevant to the copy (a conversion copywriter, a UX writer, the target customer, a brand strategist; for sales pages a skeptical buyer and an editor). Each scores 1 to 10 on their area with specific fixes; revise the lowest first; re-score until every viewpoint is at least 7 and the average at least 8. Scores here are a writing aid, never a gate status ([SKILL.md §8](../SKILL.md)).

### Quick-pass checks

| Level | Checks |
|---|---|
| Word | Cut very, really, extremely, just, actually, basically, "in order to", and vague "things" or "stuff"; use "use" for utilise, "set up" for implement, "help" for facilitate; replace innovative, robust and seamless with the fact they stand for (what it does that others do not, the failure it survives, the real number of setup steps); prefer active voice and verbs over nominalisations ("decide", not "make a decision") |
| Sentence | One idea each; vary length; put the important part first; at most three conjunctions; about 25 words or fewer |
| Paragraph | One topic; two to four sentences for the web; strong opener; logical flow; white space |

### Common problems and fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Wall of features | Add "which means..." after each |
| Corporate speak | Ask how a person would say it |
| Weak opening (company history) | Lead with the reader's problem or desired outcome |
| Buried call to action | Obvious, early, repeated |
| No proof | A specific testimonial, number or case reference, or soften the claim |
| Generic claim ("we help businesses grow") | Say who, how and by how much |
| Mixed audiences | Pick one and write to them |
| Feature overload | Three to five benefits that matter most |

### Content refresh

Refresh when traffic declines, statistics are over twelve months old, the product changed, competitors updated their version, or AI answer visibility matters. Passes: freshness (dates, statistics, examples, deprecated features), accuracy (claims, links, pricing), voice, search (has intent shifted; show a visible last-updated date), proof (newer testimonials and data), structure (comparison tables, FAQ). For a ranking page start from the results page: note the dominant format, the featured snippet and related questions; pick five competing pages; map subtopics in a grid with your page as the first column; rank what is missing as expected (four or five of five cover it: add it), common (two or three: add if it serves the reader), distinctive (one: add only if useful), or uncovered (none: your best chance to be the page worth citing). Match coverage, not word counts. Show the grid and a proposed outline marked Keep, Revise or New, offer two or three title options, and wait for approval; never promise a ranking or traffic outcome. Keep what already works: the URL (or redirect the old one permanently), heading anchors others link to, sections that rank for their own queries, and the voice.

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Core message valid, details outdated | Refresh facts, statistics and examples |
| Voice has evolved | Refresh and rewrite the voice |
| Audience or angle shifted, or structure no longer matches search intent | Full rewrite |

Cadence: pricing and product pages quarterly or on change; busy posts every six months; comparison and alternatives pages every three to six months; evergreen guides yearly unless traffic drops sooner; low-traffic pages only when data shows an opportunity.

## Implementation

1. Gather and diagnose (rules 1 to 3); state the situation in one line.
2. Draft by unit with the three-question gate (rules 4 to 6); write nothing the sources do not support (rule 9); put `[NEED: ...]` markers where proof is missing.
3. Edit: run the sweeps for situations A and B, a point edit for C; run the quick-pass checks; for high-stakes pages run the expert panel.
4. Run [prose-humanizing](prose-humanizing.md), restore any altered fact, then deliver in the output format of rule 15 with the copy saved as a file the page reads from (a content or copy file, not strings buried in components).
5. Hand open `[NEED: ...]` items to the user as action items with the evidence that would close each.

## Failure modes & fallbacks

| Failure | Fix / fallback |
|---|---|
| The brief has no proof and the user wants strong claims | Write the supportable version and list the evidence that unlocks the stronger one |
| Clean copy that is flat | Read taste from the input, add one specific detail or image per section from the source facts |
| The user's real voice gets "improved" away | Revert to the original; run the leave-it-alone test |
| A claim is true for one segment and risky for another | Segment the page or traffic ([conversion-optimization](conversion-optimization.md)) |
| A competitor could use the line unchanged | Rewrite from the differentiator, or flag `[NEED: differentiator]` |
| Copy conflicts with the layout (it does not fit) | Cut words; never shrink type below the accessible minimum ([accessibility](accessibility.md)) |

## Verification

- A one-line diagnosis was stated; point edits touched only what was asked.
- Every claim is supported by supplied material, or marked `[NEED: ...]` with the unlock named; no invented proof appears.
- No contrast reveals, negation lists, trailing pile-ons, self-answered questions or stock openers remain; headline, subhead, button and subject-line text has no em dashes.
- Prices, numbers, names, quotations and legal text match the supplied originals.
- The page has one primary action and a complete argument, and the copy lives in a file the page reads.
